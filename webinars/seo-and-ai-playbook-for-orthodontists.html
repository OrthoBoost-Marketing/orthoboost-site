Why most orthodontic websites are quietly broken

If your site isn't ranking, the reflex is to assume you did something wrong. You almost certainly didn't. Doing orthodontic SEO properly used to take real, sustained work — and most agencies were never paid enough to bother. So the technical foundation got skipped, the content stayed thin, and the site slowly became the thing holding the practice back instead of feeding it.

For years that was an expensive problem to fix. It isn't anymore. The entire category of work that agencies quietly cut — the schema, the speed, the crawl errors, the broken internal linking — is now the fastest part of the job. There's no excuse for a broken orthodontic website in 2026.

Which is exactly why the technical side stopped being the competitive advantage. Once everyone can fix the back end instantly, ranking comes down to a single question: do you have a relevant page for what people are actually searching?

Why coverage beats seniority now

The old rule in local search was simple and brutal: whoever had been around the longest, with the most history and the most reviews, took the top spot. Newer practices could work hard for years and never catch up.

That era is ending, because search doesn't reward age — it rewards relevance. A patient in Chandler searching "braces in Chandler" gets matched to the most relevant page for that query. If the practice down the street has a real page about braces in Chandler and you have a general services page that mentions six towns, they win, no matter how long you've been open.

So the play is coverage. "Braces in Phoenix." "Braces in Chandler." "Invisalign in Gilbert." Every service, crossed with every city you serve, each with its own page. Multiple offices means every location gets the full grid. Built by hand, that's hundreds of pages and a year of work. Built with AI, it's the starting point rather than the finish line.

And it compounds on its own. Every week the system scans for the searches you're missing and builds the pages to capture them, so your coverage keeps widening while you're chairside treating patients.

The second front door: getting recommended by AI

There are now two ways a patient finds you. The first is the search engine you already know about. The second is a patient typing "best orthodontist near me for Invisalign" into an AI assistant and getting back a short list of names.

That second list is not random. AI recommends the practice with the strongest, clearest public evidence — consistent business information, real reviews, and detailed pages that plainly state what you treat and where you treat it. The encouraging part is that it's the same work. The page structure that wins traditional orthodontic SEO is precisely what an AI reads when it decides which practice to name.

Send your ads to the right page, not the homepage

One more thing that quietly costs practices money: two different patients searching two different things get sent to the same homepage. A parent researching braces for a twelve-year-old and an adult comparing clear aligner pricing want completely different pages, different proof, and different next steps.

Persona-matched landing pages fix that. Each ad points to the page built for the person who clicked it, which lifts conversion without spending another dollar on traffic.

Frequently asked questions

How long does orthodontic SEO take to work?

Most practices see movement on lower-competition city and service terms within 60 to 90 days, with the bigger head terms taking six months or more. What changed in 2026 is that the page structure underneath it all can be built in days instead of months, so the clock starts far sooner than it used to.

Why is my orthodontic website not ranking on Google?

In most cases it isn't a penalty or a technical failure — it's coverage. The site has a handful of broad service pages and no page that matches the specific way patients actually search, which is service plus city. If there's no page about braces in a given suburb, there's nothing for Google to rank for that search.

Do I really need a separate page for every city I serve?

If you want to rank in those cities, yes. Search engines match a query to the most relevant page, and one services page can't be the most relevant result for a dozen different towns. Each service and city combination needs its own page with genuinely local content — which is exactly the work AI has made practical to do at scale.

How do I get my practice recommended by AI search tools?

AI assistants recommend the practice with the strongest and clearest public evidence: consistent business information, real reviews, and detailed pages that plainly state what you treat and where. The same page structure that wins traditional search is what AI reads when deciding who to name, so you're not doing the work twice.
